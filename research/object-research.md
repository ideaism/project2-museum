# Object Research: Three Political Mugs

This file is the object research handoff for The Glitching Archive. It separates verified catalogue/context information, visible evidence from supplied images, labelled interpretation, and gaps that must remain unresolved.

## Research Rules

- Use the app-facing `sourceType` values for current UI compatibility: `fact`, `inference`, `speculation`, `visitorContribution`, and `redacted`.
- Use `researchType` for finer editorial labelling: `fact`, `visible-evidence`, `historical-context`, `inference`, `speculation`, `visitor-contribution`, `unresolved`, and `redacted`.
- Surface fragments must be visible evidence, verified metadata, or explicitly labelled unresolved metadata.
- Middle fragments may use inference or speculation only.
- Core fragments should hold unresolved questions, redactions, and local visitor prompts.
- No personal testimony, ownership history, donor history, campaign context, or provenance should be invented.

## Constellation

The three mugs form a political-memory constellation rather than a generic catalogue set:

- People's March for Jobs: walking protest, unemployment, route memory.
- Support the Miners: labour conflict, strike solidarity, family/community pressure.
- Labour red rose mug: institutional political branding entering everyday life.

The shared interaction is a pour: surface record, middle reading, core uncertainty.

## Support the Miners

Current app record: `sample-mug`

Title: `Support the Miners: Coal, Justice, Future`

Verified or partly verified metadata:

- V&A East Storehouse display `Marking moments in time` includes C.174-1991, described as a mug by Commemorative Pottery, England, 1984, earthenware/glaze/glazing.
- Exact match between C.174-1991 and the supplied miners mug image is not yet confirmed.

Visible evidence from supplied image/story brief:

- White ceramic mug.
- Black printed miners imagery.
- Visible inscriptions: `BRITAIN'S COAL FOR BRITAIN'S FUTURE`, `SUPPORT THE MINERS`, `STRIKING FOR JUSTICE 1972 1974 1984`.

Historical context used cautiously:

- The printed years align with miners strike contexts in 1972, 1974, and 1984-85.
- Museum Wales / Big Pit states that the National Coal Board announced planned closures of 20 coal mines with 20,000 job losses in March 1984.

Research gaps:

- Confirm exact V&A object match.
- Confirm maker, accession number, production run, and production context.
- Confirm whether the mug was sold, gifted, or used for fundraising.
- Confirm rights for image and model use.
- Do not infer owner, family story, local dispute, or coalfield testimony.

## People's March for Jobs

Current app record: `commemorative-protest-mug`

Title: `People's March for Jobs: Route Held in a Cup`

Verified or partly verified metadata:

- V&A East Storehouse display `Marking moments in time` lists `Peoples March for Jobs`, museum number C.106-1991, maker unknown, England, 1981, earthenware/firing.
- The supplied image is treated as a likely but still unconfirmed match to C.106-1991.

Visible evidence from supplied image/story brief:

- Beige or speckled ceramic mug.
- Green typography, border, route-map graphic, and town names.
- Visible inscriptions: `Peoples march for jobs`, `Liverpool-London May 1981`.

Historical context used cautiously:

- People's History Museum describes the 1981 People's March for Jobs as a Liverpool-to-London protest.
- TUC Library states the Liverpool march left on 1 May 1981 and reached London on 31 May.
- Hackney Museum records a related `Peoples march for jobs` badge and links the campaign to unemployment and youth participants.

Research gaps:

- Confirm supplied image against V&A C.106-1991.
- Transcribe the full route and small town names from high-resolution imagery.
- Confirm whether the mug was sold, issued, gifted, or collected after the march.
- Confirm full image rights and display permissions.
- Do not invent marcher names, host locations, oral histories, or protest outcomes.

Title: `Labour Red Rose: Party Name, Domestic Signal`

Verified or partly verified metadata:

- V&A East Storehouse display `Marking moments in time` includes C.91-1991, described as a mug, maker unknown, Staffordshire, possibly 1986, earthenware/glaze/printed/glazed.
- Exact match to the supplied Labour image is not confirmed.

Visible evidence from supplied image/story brief:

- The full design, date, maker, campaign, and provenance are not visible from the supplied crop.

Historical context used cautiously:

- V&A frames factory-produced mugs as everyday objects for graphic design and messaging.
- Brighton & Hove Museums frames popular pottery as everyday objects that can comment on social and political history.

Research gaps:

- Confirm whether the supplied image matches V&A C.91-1991.
